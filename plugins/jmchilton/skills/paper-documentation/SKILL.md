# Paper documentation

Work from the study's data, analysis, and cited sources. Use the target venue's requirements when supplied.

- Build a small fact ledger before drafting: result, units, denominator or sample size, comparison, uncertainty, and source artifact. Keep observations separate from interpretations.
- Make Methods reproducible at the level the paper requires: inputs, procedure, software or instrument versions where relevant, parameters, and analysis choices.
- Give a Results paragraph a clear finding, the supporting measurement, and its interpretation. Explain the mechanism behind a result where the evidence permits. Do not imply causation, generality, or certainty beyond the evidence.
- Let the abstract move from context and gap to approach, main evidence, and takeaway. Keep it accessible to the paper's wider audience; put specialized implementation detail in Methods.
- Define specialized terms by their function on first use. Avoid piling up acronyms, formula names, and tool names before the reader knows what happens to the data.
- Make figure captions usable on their own: identify panels, groups, units, visual encodings, sample sizes, tests, and error bars when relevant. Check captions against the figures and main text.
- Verify bibliographic details against the publication or a reliable bibliographic record. Do not invent references or silently replace uncertain citations.

Before delivery, check that numbers agree across text, tables, figures, and abstract. Remove unsupported superlatives and conclusions that only promise future impact. Check unresolved citations and venue limits; list evidence gaps that need the author's decision.

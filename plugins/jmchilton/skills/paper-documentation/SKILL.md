# Paper documentation

Work from the study's data, analysis, and cited sources. Use the target venue's requirements when supplied.

## John's hard rules

- No semicolons in prose. Use a period, a closed em-dash, or a comma. Preserve punctuation inside quotations, citations, code, and data.
- Use closed em-dashes (`—`) for appositive naming and load-bearing asides, without surrounding spaces. Use them where they help the sentence, not as decoration.
- Keep the abstract, summary, and introduction hook conceptual and accessible. Introduce technical nouns in Methods and Results when they help explain the work.
- Keep a metaphor only when it explains something the literal sentence cannot. Prefer concrete evidence to decorative flourishes.
- In original prose, avoid `ships` and `ships today`, `substrate` as technology jargon, `uses rather than owns`, and `gate` for a human decision. Name the actual behavior, infrastructure, ownership arrangement, or approval step instead. Literal, technical, and quoted uses can remain.

## Draft and verify

- Build a small fact ledger before drafting: result, units, denominator or sample size, comparison, uncertainty, and source artifact. Keep observations separate from interpretations.
- Make Methods reproducible at the level the paper requires: inputs, procedure, software or instrument versions where relevant, parameters, and analysis choices.
- Keep the scientific narrative moving. In Results, state the finding, give its measurement, then explain the analysis or mechanism that makes it credible. In Methods, say what was done and why a choice matters. In Discussion, interpret the evidence and its limits. Cut generic scene-setting, nested passive clauses, and jargon that delays the point. Every sentence should add a result, method, causal link, qualification, or consequence. Do not imply causation or certainty beyond the evidence.
- Use decisive agency without hype. Name the actor and the completed action: `we evaluated`, `we estimated`, `we quantified`, or `we calibrated` when those verbs match the work. Also use precise verbs such as `resolved`, `restored`, `inflated`, `conflated`, `penalized`, `decoupled`, `saturated`, and `recovered` when the result supports them. Cut empty hedges such as `we attempt to explore`, `it might suggest`, and `could potentially indicate`. State uncertainty through the estimate, interval, study design, or a specific limitation instead of timid phrasing. Never strengthen a claim beyond the evidence to make it sound decisive.
- Let the abstract move from context and gap to approach, main evidence, and takeaway. Keep it accessible to the paper's wider audience. Put specialized implementation detail in Methods.
- Define specialized terms by their function on first use. Avoid piling up acronyms, formula names, and tool names before the reader knows what happens to the data.
- Make figure captions usable on their own: identify panels, groups, units, visual encodings, sample sizes, tests, and error bars when relevant. Check captions against the figures and main text.
- Verify bibliographic details against the publication or a reliable bibliographic record. Do not invent references or silently replace uncertain citations.

Before delivery, check that numbers agree across text, tables, figures, and abstract. Remove unsupported superlatives and conclusions that only promise future impact. Check unresolved citations and venue limits. List evidence gaps that need the author's decision.
